How to read a cron expression without guessing

Cron's five fields decide when a job runs, and they are easy to misread. Here is how to work out what a line means, and how to check it before you rely on it.

The five fields

A standard cron line has five fields, separated by spaces: minute, hour, day of the month, month, and day of the week. A number in a field means "at this value", and a star means "every". So 0 9 * * * reads as nine in the morning, every day.

The symbols that trip people up

Three symbols do most of the work:

  • A star (*) means every value of that field.
  • A slash sets a step. */15 in the minute field is every fifteen minutes.
  • A dash sets a range and a comma sets a list. 1-5 in the day-of-week field is Monday to Friday; 1,15 in the day-of-month field is the 1st and the 15th.

The field that catches people out is day of the week, where both 0 and 7 mean Sunday.

Check it before you rely on it

The safest habit is to read the schedule back in plain words and look at the next few run times before you save it. Paste the line into the cron explainer and it does both: a sentence describing when it runs, and the upcoming dates. If the sentence does not match what you meant, the expression is wrong, not your memory.
