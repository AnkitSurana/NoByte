How to compress an image without it going blurry

Compression trades file size against detail. Here is how to make an image smaller while keeping it looking right, and how to choose the format.

Why smaller tends to mean softer

JPG and WebP save space by discarding the detail your eye is least likely to miss. Push the quality down far enough and the losses start to show as soft edges and blocky patches in areas of flat colour. The trick is finding the point just before that begins.

Using the slider

Open the image compressor and drop an image in. Move the quality slider down and watch the file size fall next to a live preview. Somewhere around the middle, most photos lose more than half their size with no difference you can spot at normal viewing distance. When the preview starts to look worse, step back up a little.

Which format, and when

JPG suits photographs. WebP usually produces a smaller file at the same quality and handles both photos and graphics, so it is worth trying wherever the place you are uploading to accepts it. For a screenshot with sharp text, keep the quality high; text is where compression shows first.
